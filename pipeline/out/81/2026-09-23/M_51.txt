Mr President, 

Mr Secretary General, 

Ladies and gentlemen, 

Allow me to congratulate Mr. Khalilur Rahman on his election as President of the 81st session of the United Nations General Assembly and wish him every success in this responsible mission. 

Today, the world needs not only more dialogue, but also more trust, more responsibility and more capacity to act together. Multilateralism only makes sense if it leads to decisions and if decisions lead to actions and results. 

Man must be at the heart of this common response: his life, his dignity, his rights and his possibilities. This requires an international order based on the Charter of the United Nations and international law, and a United Nations that not only formulates principles, but has the power and capacity to respect them. An organization that defends multilateralism, and that means a world order of rules for all, for small and large countries without double standards. 

Bulgaria will continue to be a constant defender of this order and of a strong, effective and active UN. 

Ladies and gentlemen, 

Today, the UN must prove not only its ability to bring states together, but also its ability to act – through joint efforts, it is our duty to transform the world of disorder into a world of international law and lasting peace. The pact for the future provides important direction, but this must be backed by real reform. The Security Council must reflect today's world, with fairer regional representation and greater legitimacy and effectiveness. Bulgaria supports its expansion to both categories of membership, including at least one additional non-permanent seat for the Eastern European Group. 

The UN80 initiative is an opportunity to make the Organization more efficient, better coordinated and better able to fulfill its mandates. But reform without resources is not possible. Predictable and sustainable funding is necessary for the UN to fulfill the responsibilities that States entrust to it.

The upcoming election of a new Secretary General will also be a choice for the direction of the Organization. We thank Antonio Guterres for his dedication to serving the United Nations. We expect his successor to have the vision, courage and leadership to rebuild trust, overcome divisions and act for change. 

Your Excellencies, 

Peace and security are among the main responsibilities of the international community. Faced with conflicts that have cost thousands of lives and caused enormous destruction, we must redouble our efforts to prevent and end hostilities. 

The main challenge remains the war against Ukraine, the consequences of which extend far beyond the immediate combat zone. 

Is it not time to rethink European security policy? Bulgaria, as an EU member state, raises its voice and proposes that we also have a debate on this topic and the broader goal of the future of post-war Europe. And to begin the debate, it is high time to transfer the conflict from the army to the political and diplomatic domain. These are negotiations, which require dialogue and at least a minimum of trust, supported by appropriate guarantees. The fate of Ukraine cannot be decided without Ukraine. But Europe cannot be a mere observer, it must be a real participant in the negotiations on the post-war security architecture on the continent after the end of the war. Europe must be able to guarantee its own security. Military security should be complemented by the second pillar of security, the contractual-legal pillar, which should ensure that the strengthening of defense potential brings more security rather than a new arms race. For the security of Europe, we must add strong diplomacy to military and economic security. 

It would be irresponsible if, after the end of the war in Ukraine, we could not offer European citizens anything better than a new and worse Cold War. We need a vision for the future according to the formula “a Europe of peace thanks to dissuasive defense and shared security”. From this highest platform we make a call to guide us on the path to this goal!

Bulgaria considers the security and stability of the Black Sea region to be an integral part of its national security in all its dimensions. The war between Russia and Ukraine has moved beyond the territorial waters of the parties to the conflict, which directly affects Bulgaria today. However, the Black Sea problem has moved from regional to global because, with the regional negative consequences combined with the war in Iran and the aridity of the Danube, it leads to a food crisis in the world, mainly in the Middle East and Africa. The inability to export cereals and fertilizers poses a real risk of famine this winter in a number of countries in these regions and of compromising the harvest in 2027. 

Bulgaria cannot and does not want to remain indifferent and, applying the approach of practical diplomacy, is preparing an initiative for which it will strive, together with the countries of the region and all interested parties, to reach an agreement and joint action in order to ensure freedom/safety of navigation in the Black Sea. We are convinced that this subject deserves the attention of the United Nations. 

Bulgaria therefore demands that the Black Sea be transformed from an area of ​​increased risk and threat into a zone of “shared security” – a security zone, a zone of free transport, energy, trade, digital corridors, tourism, a zone where different cultures, religions and political systems meet in peace. 

The Middle East also needs political solutions and de-escalation. 

A new cycle of destruction, humanitarian crisis and violence cannot be allowed in Gaza. It is necessary to create the conditions for stabilization and reconstruction, credible security guarantees for Israel and effective Palestinian governance. Bulgaria condemns the terrorist actions of Hamas and insists on its complete and unconditional disarmament. The organization has no place in the governance of Gaza. We remain convinced that the only lasting solution is for two States, Israel and Palestine, living side by side in peace and security. This is the only way to give both peoples the prospect of a dignified life and a peaceful future. 

Political decisions to achieve stability must also be at the forefront of Lebanon, Syria and Yemen.

In the context of developments around Iran, the security of global sea lanes and, first of all, the route through the Strait of Hormuz have highlighted the global dimensions of this issue. As we have publicly emphasized on several occasions in international fora, Bulgaria strongly supports the freedom and security of international maritime transport and insists on full compliance with applicable international law and the United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea. 

There are also major challenges in Africa. In Sudan, the Sahel and the Great Lakes region, political processes, protection of civilians and strengthening of state institutions, long-term development efforts and economic opportunities are needed. 

Security requires that we limit the risks arising from weapons of mass destruction and their proliferation. Bulgaria remains committed to strengthening the international architecture of non-proliferation, disarmament and arms control and the three pillars of the Treaty on the Non-Proliferation of Nuclear Weapons. We support the objective of a world without them, which must be pursued through pragmatic measures: more transparency, verifiable control and limitation of arsenals. 

Global conflicts have introduced new, previously unknown threats, even threatening nuclear security through reckless attacks on nuclear sites and vital lines of support for their safety, such as attacks on the Zaporizhzhia nuclear power plant and other Ukrainian nuclear power plants. 

On the other hand, compliance with the Non-Proliferation Treaty is the essential condition for Iran, which must above all strictly comply with its international obligations and reach a lasting nuclear agreement under the control of the International Atomic Energy Agency. 

We called repeatedly and it remains valid to this day: The DPRK should cease its nuclear and missile programs.

Today's security requires that we act on time. This implies a strengthening of diplomacy, mediation and political dialogue, but also effective control of the proliferation of weapons and the military application of new technologies. Artificial intelligence must be developed within the framework of international law and under effective human control, and space must remain a space of peaceful and safe use. 

The same principle must guide our response to terrorism, radicalization, disinformation and cyber threats. No country can do it alone. Strong institutions, effective UN peacekeeping operations and closer international cooperation are needed. Bulgaria will continue to defend this approach. Sustainable security is built not only by responding to crises, but also by our ability to prevent them. 

Your Excellencies, 

As a member of the United Nations Human Rights Council in 2024-2026, Bulgaria works towards a public environment where discrimination, racism, anti-Semitism and hate speech have no place. Media freedom, the protection of journalists and access to reliable information are an essential part of this environment, particularly in the face of growing disinformation. 

This commitment also includes ensuring that everyone has the opportunity to participate and be heard. Children's rights and access to education, the equal participation of women and girls in societal processes and the full inclusion of young people in these are essential to build societies in which everyone is part of the decisions that determine their future. 

Bulgaria supports concrete international efforts in this direction, from guaranteeing the right to education of every child to the coherent implementation of the Women, Peace and Security Agenda. The protection of human rights is measured not only by commitments made, but also by real opportunities for participation, education and a dignified life. 

This is our common goal: societies in which differences do not divide, hatred does not define and the rights and dignity of every person are protected. 

Ladies and Gentlemen,

Sustainable development is not an abstract objective. This means enabling people to live with dignity, receive quality education and healthcare, have jobs and prospects, and enable societies to respond to crises and change without leaving anyone behind. Therefore, Bulgaria remains committed to implementing the 2030 Agenda for Sustainable Development and accelerating progress in achieving the Sustainable Development Goals. 

Environmental responsibility is one of the fundamental principles of sustainable development. Unfortunately for us, in the context of the war against Ukraine, such a topic is becoming more and more pressing. The resilience and balance of the Black Sea marine ecosystems are threatened by the harmful consequences of the military conflict. Bulgaria urges political commitment and compliance with UN General Assembly Resolution 77/104 adopted on December 7, 2022 on the protection of the environment in armed conflicts. 

For Bulgaria, investment in education, science and culture is an investment in the long-term viability of societies. Not only do they preserve knowledge and identity, they give people the ability to adapt and create. Bulgaria will continue to respect this agreement also at the international level. Our election as a member of the Intergovernmental Committee for the Safeguarding of UNESCO's Intangible Cultural Heritage for the period 2026-2030 is an opportunity to make this commitment an even more active contribution. 

Sustainability means being ready for changes that are already underway. The climate is changing, health crises can affect the entire world and new technologies are changing the way we work and live. We can't stop change, but we have to manage it to make it work for people. This means a just ecological transition, better global connectivity and preparation for a common response to health crises. The help of artificial intelligence will be indispensable if it develops our capabilities without undermining security and trust. Bulgaria will continue to contribute to the international dialogue on this topic, notably by participating in the preparation of the second global dialogue on AI governance in 2027.

Your Excellencies,

All these efforts lead to a common goal, to a world in which peace, security, human rights, and development are supported and complemented, and where international law protects human dignity.

Let us ensure that the UN is not only a forum to discuss crises, but a driving force for their prevention and peaceful resolution.

Because the future does not necessarily belong to the strongest, it will belong to those who succeed in building trust.

There is no greater responsibility to our peoples than to deliver to future generations a world in which cooperation is stronger than confrontation, dialogue stronger than hate speech, and peace stronger than war. And Bulgaria is ready to contribute to this not only as a security user, but also as a security creator.
