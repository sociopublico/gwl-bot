New York, September 23, 2026 (check with the speech delivered) Ladies and gentlemen, I stand on this platform with one objective: to defend what many are trying to destroy. 

I come to reclaim the principles and ambitions that inspired the creation of this organization, 81 years ago. Principles and ambitions that several governments present here despise and seek to dismantle. 

Eight years have passed since I first addressed this House. Since then, several things have changed in the world. And I'm sorry to say that many have made it worse. 

In 2018, the 193 countries that make up this organization had just approved the 2030 Agenda and the majority of leaders present here proudly wore the symbol of the SDGs. Not because it was fashionable. Not because we thought it would be an easy path. We did it because most of us believed in their common promise. Because we were convinced that peace, sustainability, and equal opportunities are not ideological positions, but an integral part of the future of humanity. 

Today, however, these ideas are not only questioned. They are being attacked by reactionary entities and governments that want to return us to a gray past. There is no need to point them out. We all know who they are and what their modus operandi is. 

They start by questioning the rules. Then they fail to comply, knowing that no one will dare to hold them accountable. They cut their contributions to multilateral organizations like this one. They point to their leaders and workers. They block their ability to act, and then describe them as useless entities that we can do without. 

Theirs is a planned and coordinated demolition attempt that is presented to us as something unstoppable and necessary. But in reality it goes against the structural currents of history and is built on three wrong pillars. 

The first is selfishness. The absurd belief that the world is a zero-sum game in which the gain of some necessarily implies the loss of others. 

The second is ignorance. A colossal and shameless ignorance, which despises science and assumes that there are countries so powerful that they can abuse the rest and do not need anyone to prosper. 

And the third, the worst of all, is cruelty. A cruelty that no longer even flinches when seeing the ribs of a starving child or a hospital reduced to rubble.

Throughout these eight years I have seen these reactionary forces – selfish, ignorant and cruel – grow. I have seen how they took off their masks and seized power in many places. And from there, I have seen them launch illegal wars and massacre innocent civilians. I have seen them treat millions of human beings as if they were cattle and withdraw their help from those who suffer the most from poverty and disease. I have seen them pollute the atmosphere and the ocean as if they were their property. And conspire to destroy this organization and everything it represents. Witnessing these things has transformed my way of understanding the world. But it hasn't made me lose hope. Because, throughout these eight years, I have also seen wonderful things that make me continue to trust in humanity. 

I have seen millions of people mobilize in defense of nature, peace and civil rights. 

I have seen thousands of activists and public servants stand up to reactionary powers, knowing that they were putting their careers and their own lives in danger. I have seen scientists achieve advances that we thought were impossible. And many governments do the right thing: betting on cooperation, social justice and environmental sustainability. All of this has taught me that humanity is neither intrinsically good nor bad. 

Humanity can be both. And every year, every day, in every crisis, you must decide how to act. 

The United Nations is one of the spaces where this decision is materialized at the highest level. 

He doesn't always get it right. Of course not. I myself have pointed out on several occasions the contradictions and shortcomings of this organization. But, with the same conviction, I must denounce that the majority of the voices that today insist on criticizing him are not describing his failure: they are trying to provoke it. 

And I must also say that multilateralism is the best instrument that humanity has to manage its diversity and interdependence, especially in a globalized and multipolar world like the current one. 

Therefore, what we have to do is redouble our trust in him. We must renew and strengthen it. So that it can play in this century the same central role that it played in the last century. 

Doing so will not be easy. But the good news is that all the guidance we need is here.

In this Letter that our ancestors wrote. Men and women who had seen the world burn and who – still – had the courage to imagine a better future. 

Those men and women left their wisdom and their hope in this letter. And they did NOT ask us to keep it in a display case. They asked us to comply. That we defended her. And that we would return to it in moments of uncertainty and difficulty. 

Today we are going through one of those moments. And it is our duty to live up to the courage and ambition present in this text. 

That is why we have to reform the Security Council. So that all regions of the world have fair representation. And to end the veto right of the permanent members. Because, as this Charter indicates, no country should be judge and party at the same time. No matter how powerful it is. 

We have to strengthen the rest of the Main Bodies and reorganize the Agencies, Funds and Programs, providing them with more resources and executive capacity. In order for them to have a more decisive impact on the great challenges facing humanity, we must strengthen organizations such as the International Criminal Court, so that they can investigate, prosecute and punish those who violate the rules. It took humanity centuries to replace the right of the strongest with the force of law and we cannot be the generation that travels that path in the opposite direction. 

For that same reason, we have to rebuild the architecture of nuclear non-proliferation and disarmament, to prevent a poorly planned security policy from dragging us into a new world conflict. 

And we have to impose strict regulation on the techno-oligarchs that controls the functioning of social networks and the development of Artificial Intelligence. Because the future of our children has to be above that of their pockets. 

Doing all of these things will require commitment. Time. And strong leadership. Able to build agreements. To listen. To look beyond the known reality and imagine a better alternative. 

My bet for that leadership is clear. Nine men have held the General Secretariat of this Organization in its eighty years of life. Now it's a woman's turn. To a Latin American woman. 

Because – ladies and gentlemen – how can an Organization that claims to speak on behalf of all humanity never have had a woman at its head?

How is it going to represent all the continents, if for three decades we have not allowed the third most populated continent to occupy this position? 

The United Nations was born with the promise that all human beings would have a place in this house. The time has come to fulfill the pending half. The time has come for a woman from Latin America to occupy the General Secretary. This is a historical debt that we must pay off. Today, more than ever, when feminism is being attacked and thousands of women are victims of machismo, violence and xenophobia. 

And I want to be clear: I'm not saying this because I think women have anything to prove. 

I say this because it is the world that needs to demonstrate to itself that on the path to equality there is no turning back. 

In my country, Spain, women were the first to show that security does not come from strength, but from social progress. 

They were brave women, like Clara Campoamor, who were the first to defend in Parliament the renunciation of war as a way of relating to the world and the commitment to empathy and peace. I wish that many of the leaders who have risen to this lectern throughout this week had half the courage and lucidity that she had. 

Feminism is one of the main forces of transformation and progress that the world has known in the last century and it is time for its force to guide this institution. For these and other reasons, I believe that, to enter on the right foot into this new decisive stage that they are entering, the United Nations will need a Latin American Secretary General. 

And I believe that, in order for her to not be alone, she will in turn need the support of brave governments willing to do the right thing. No matter how high the price to pay. 

That is the path that Spain has chosen. 

In the last eight years, my country has opposed ALL wars of aggression wherever they have been unleashed. Whether in Ukraine, Lebanon or Iran. 

We have refused to increase our military spending to 5%. And we have done everything in our power to avoid attacks contrary to the Charter of the United Nations. Sanctioning Russian aggressors. Imposing an arms embargo on Israel. Preventing our territory from being used to deploy aggression contrary to international legality. And condemning without nuance the genocide that Netanyahu continues to perpetrate in Gaza.

We have also mobilized resources to do more. And in the face of the chainsaw that has cut almost a third of global development aid in just two years, Spain has doubled its investment in cooperation and has increased its contribution to the multilateral system by 60%. And we have done it while always being faithful to our principles. 

Not even in times as difficult as the one Ceuta is experiencing today, where inequality, desperation and misinformation have fueled one of the biggest irregular migration crises in recent years. 

I know that, in times like this, the temptation is to choose the shortcut. Give in to fear, turn the migrant into an enemy and violate legality. But Spain is not going to do it. And neither does Ceuta. Because its citizens are demonstrating that a crisis can be faced without giving up decency, maintaining legality and respecting human rights. And we will come out of it stronger precisely because of that. Because Spain does not choose the easiest path. Choose the fairest path. 

The whole world knows it. And the reactionary forces that want to destroy the multilateral order do not stop attacking us for this. They want us to cower. They want us to surrender. They want us to end up believing that the SDGs are lost causes. That defending the common good is naive. 

That cooperating is an act of weakness. 

Well then. I am here to tell you – loud and clear – that you are wrong and that we are NOT going to remain silent. 

We Spaniards are NOT going to take a step back. We are NOT going to stop believing that humanity can continue to progress and that, together, we can build a better world. 

And we are not going to do it, because we want to fulfill our sons and daughters. Because we know that, in a few years, future generations will look back. They will study this decisive decade. 

And they will ask us what role each of us plays. They will ask us what we did when some countries started breaking the rules and others decided to turn a blind eye. They will ask us what we did when defending what we believed in began to cost us dearly. And, when that happens, we want to be able to tell them that we DON'T stop fighting. We do NOT. We want to be able to tell them that we are working hard to leave them a more peaceful, more sustainable and fairer world. 

That's what we hope to tell you. And that is the commitment that I want to leave on this platform. 

Not in my name, but in that of the country I represent. 

Thank you so much.
